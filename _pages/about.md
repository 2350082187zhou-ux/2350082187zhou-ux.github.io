<h2 id="about-me" data-home-i18n="aboutTitle">About Me</h2>

<p data-home-i18n="introEducation">I received my Bachelor's degree in <strong>Electrical Engineering and Automation</strong> from <strong>Anhui Polytechnic University</strong>, Wuhu, China, in 2025. I am currently pursuing a <strong>Ph.D. in Computer Science</strong> at the <strong>University of Nevada, Reno (UNR)</strong>, USA, under the supervision of <a href="https://www.unr.edu/cse/people/niusen-chen">Dr. Niusen Chen</a>.</p>

<p data-home-i18n="introResearch">My research interests include <strong>computer systems security, operating systems, storage systems, AI agent security, and hardware security</strong>. My current research investigates the security risks posed by AI agents with elevated privileges, evaluates host-side protection mechanisms at the file-system, kernel, and block-device layers, and explores stronger security boundaries for protecting sensitive data against unauthorized modification and deletion.</p>

<h2 id="selected-research-project" data-home-i18n="projectTitle">Selected Research Project</h2>

<h3 id="when-ai-agents-go-rogue-who-protects-your-files" data-home-i18n="projectName">When AI Agents Go Rogue: Who Protects Your Files?</h3>

<p data-home-i18n="projectDesc">In this project, I investigate whether host-controlled protection mechanisms can safeguard sensitive files when an AI agent has root privileges. I examine defenses at different levels of the software stack, including <strong>AppArmor</strong>, <strong>eBPF LSM</strong>, and <strong>block-layer enforcement</strong>, to identify the limits of host-side protection and motivate stronger data-integrity boundaries.</p>
